Why I Stopped Using Just One AI — And What I Actually Use Now

May 11, 2026  ·  6 min read  ·  AI Tool Compare

For about a year, I used ChatGPT for everything. Writing, research, brainstorming, summarizing documents, drafting emails, trying to understand my health insurance explanation of benefits. Everything. It was just easier to have one tab open and one mental model of what I was working with.

I think a lot of people do this. You find a tool that works, you get comfortable with it, and the idea of learning another one feels like more trouble than it's worth. ChatGPT was good enough. Why fix what isn't broken?

Then a few things happened that made me start testing alternatives — and I realized I'd been leaving a lot on the table.

The Email That Started It

I had to write a difficult email. Someone I'd worked with for years had been consistently dropping the ball on a shared project, and I needed to address it directly without blowing up a relationship I wanted to keep. I asked ChatGPT to help. The output was fine. Professional, inoffensive, covered the main points.

A colleague mentioned she'd been using Claude for writing. I figured I'd try it with the same prompt, mostly out of curiosity. The difference was noticeable enough that I actually stopped and read it twice. The Claude version felt like something a thoughtful person would actually write. It understood the emotional nuance of the situation — the thing I was trying to do was be honest without being cruel, and it got that.

I sent the Claude version.

This isn't a knock on ChatGPT. It's very good. But "very good at everything" and "best for this specific thing" are different, and I'd been treating them as the same.

Then the Research Problem

A few weeks later, I needed to dig into some information about a topic where the details mattered and being wrong would have been embarrassing. I asked ChatGPT, got a confident-sounding answer, and used it. I later found out one of the key claims was just... off. Not wildly wrong, but wrong enough that I had to go back and correct it.

I started using Perplexity for anything factual. Every answer comes with citations you can actually click. If something is wrong or outdated, you can see where it came from and judge for yourself. The experience of using it for research is so different from ChatGPT that it's almost a different category of tool.

ChatGPT sounds confident about everything. Perplexity shows you its sources. For anything where accuracy matters, that difference is not small.

What I Use Now (And Why)

I've settled into a workflow that uses four tools. This sounds like more overhead than it is — each tool has a clear job, and I rarely have to think about which one to reach for.

🗂 My current stack

Claude
Writing anything that goes to another human. Emails, proposals, anything where tone and nuance matter. Also my go-to for thinking through complex problems — it pushes back more than ChatGPT does.
ChatGPT
Brainstorming and generating options. When I need 10 ideas and I only need 1 to be good, ChatGPT produces more variety. Also for image generation and anything where I need a lot of output fast.
Perplexity
Research. Full stop. Anything factual where I need to be able to verify the source. I now run most "what is X" or "what does Y do" questions through Perplexity before I trust the answer.
DeepSeek
Backup for high-volume days when I've hit limits on the others, or for quick questions that don't need the full power of Claude or ChatGPT. It's free and it's better than I expected.

The Part People Don't Want to Hear

The honest version of why most people stick with one AI tool isn't that it's the best one for everything — it's that switching has a cost. You have to learn the new interface, rebuild your mental model of what it's good at, and accept that some prompts that work in ChatGPT need tweaking in Claude.

That cost is real. But in my experience it's about a week of adjustment before the new tool stops feeling foreign. And after that week, the upgrade in output quality for the right tasks is worth it.

What I'd Tell Someone Just Starting Out

Don't start with the tool everyone uses. Start with the task you most need help with, and find the tool that's actually best for it.

If you write a lot — proposals, emails, anything client-facing — try Claude. If you do research and accuracy matters — try Perplexity. If you need to brainstorm or generate a lot of variations fast — ChatGPT is still excellent at that. If you just want something free and surprisingly capable for everyday questions — DeepSeek.

You don't have to use four tools. Even switching one thing — using Perplexity instead of ChatGPT for research, or Claude instead of ChatGPT for important writing — is a meaningful upgrade.

The quick version:

  • Best for writing quality: Claude
  • Best for brainstorming: ChatGPT
  • Best for research with sources: Perplexity
  • Best free option: DeepSeek
  • Best for images: Midjourney (if you're paying), Leonardo AI (if you're not)
